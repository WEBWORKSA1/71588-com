Why 4 Is Unlucky — and How to Handle It

四 (sì) sounds like 死 (sǐ), "death". The consequences are very real.

The sound behind the fear

In Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and Korean, the word for four sounds like the word for death. The association — sometimes called tetraphobia — is strongest in Chinese-speaking communities and shows up in everyday decisions.

Where you'll see it

  • Buildings: many skip floors 4, 14, 24 and sometimes 13 (for Western guests), jumping straight to 5 or 15.
  • Phone numbers & plates: numbers with 4s are often cheaper or hard to sell.
  • Prices: only a tiny share of Chinese retail prices end in 4.
  • Gifts: sets of four are avoided; so are clocks (送钟 sounds like attending a funeral).

Practical workarounds

  1. Real estate: relabel floors (e.g. 3A) where permitted; price units on 4-floors competitively.
  2. Retail: remove 4 from prices, SKUs, promo codes and pack sizes.
  3. Phone lines: choose endings in 8, 6 or 9; check with the Analyzer.
  4. Events: avoid the 4th, 14th and 24th for launches aimed at Chinese-speaking audiences.

Exceptions

Context can flip meaning: some zodiac traditions list 4 among lucky numbers for certain signs, and slang like 9420 (就是爱你, "it's you I love") uses 4 playfully. Still, for mass-market audiences, avoiding 4 is the safe default.
