Why numbers have meaning

Mandarin and Cantonese have relatively few distinct syllables, so many words sound alike. Numbers inherit the meanings of the words they resemble. 八 (bā, eight) sounds close to 发 (fā, to prosper); 四 (sì, four) sounds close to 死 (sǐ, death). Over centuries these homophones became a shared cultural code used in prices, addresses, phone numbers, gifts and dates.

Famous combinations

  • 168 一路发 — "prosperity all the way". A favourite for shop phone numbers and prices.
  • 518 我要发 — "I will prosper". Basis for 5188 and our own 71588 (起要我发发).
  • 888 / 8888 — triple and quadruple prosperity.
  • 66 六六大顺 — "everything goes smoothly".
  • 99 久久 — "forever"; popular at weddings.
  • 520 我爱你 / 1314 一生一世 — "I love you" / "for a lifetime". May 20 became an unofficial Valentine's Day.
  • 250 二百五 — "idiot". Never give 250 in a red envelope or use it as a price.
  • 14, 44, 74, 514 — combinations built on 4 are avoided.

Regional differences

Meanings shift across dialects. In Cantonese, 3 (saam) resembles 生 (life) and 9 is strongly positive, while some speakers hear 5 as 唔 ("not"), turning 58 into "won't prosper". In Mandarin, 7 can mean "rise" (起) or "together" (齐), yet the seventh lunar month is Ghost Month. Always consider who your audience is.

Putting it to use

  1. Choosing a phone number: prioritise the last four digits; avoid 4s.
  2. Pricing: end prices in 8, 88 or 68 — see our pricing playbook.
  3. Gifts: give in pairs or eights, never in fours. See red envelope amounts.
