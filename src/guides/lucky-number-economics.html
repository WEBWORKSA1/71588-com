The Economics of Lucky Numbers

Superstition or not, the market prices it in. Here's the evidence.

1. Phone numbers: ¥2.33 million for 8888-8888

In August 2003, Sichuan Airlines paid ¥2.33 million (about US$280,000) for the Chengdu phone number 8888-8888. The same auction of 100 special numbers raised ¥7.13 million in total. (China Daily)

2. License plates: Hong Kong's multimillion-dollar auctions

Hong Kong's Transport Department auctions vehicle registration marks. The plate "18" sold for HK$16.5 million in 2008 and "28" (易发, "easy prosperity" in Cantonese) for HK$18.1 million (≈US$2.3M) in 2016. (CNN)

3. IPO prices: 8 is six times more common than 4

A study of Chinese A-share IPOs (1999–2009) found 19.2% of offer prices ended in 8 versus 3.3% in 4 — against an expected 10% each if random. When managers had more pricing freedom, the share ending in 8 rose to 22.5%. (Sun & Schneider)

4. Property: faster sales on 8th floors

Research on Chengdu housing (2004–06) found resale apartments on floors ending in 8 sold at a premium of about ¥235/m², and new units on those floors sold 6.9 days faster; buyers whose own phone numbers contained more 8s were more likely to buy "lucky" floors. (Shum, Sun & Ye, J. Comparative Economics, 2014)

5. Retail prices: ~40% end in 8

Analyses of advertised prices found roughly 40% ended in 8 in Chinese and Taiwanese ads versus only 1.4% in 4. (Psychology Today, citing Simmons & Schindler)

6. The 2008 Olympics

Beijing opened the Games at 8:08 pm on 08-08-08, and couples rushed to marry that day. (NPR)

7. Numeric domains

All-digit .com domains are a distinct asset class driven largely by Chinese buyers. Short, 4-free, 8-rich strings attract the most demand, while random strings trade near wholesale floors. (NamePros)

What it means for you

  • If you sell to Chinese-speaking customers, numbers are part of your brand.
  • If you own a lucky number, it may have real resale value — get a free valuation.
  • Before buying, check for 4s: they reduce liquidity.
