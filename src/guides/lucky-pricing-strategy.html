Lucky Pricing Strategy

Western retail ends prices in 9. Chinese retail ends them in 8. Here's how to use that.

The core rule

Roughly four in ten prices in Chinese and Taiwanese advertising end in 8, versus about 1.4% ending in 4 (Simmons & Schindler). An ending of 8 reads as 发 — prosperity — for buyer and seller alike.

The playbook

  1. Swap 9 → 8. $49 → $48 is tempting, but it contains a 4. Prefer $38, $58 or $68.
  2. Use signature endings: 8, 88, 68, 168, 188, 288, 888. Premium items: 1,688 / 2,888 / 8,888.
  3. Remove every 4. 4, 14, 24, 44 and 74 anywhere in a price or SKU.
  4. Never price at 250 (二百五, "fool").
  5. Bundle in 2, 6, 8 or 9 — never 4 — especially for gifts and mooncake sets.
  6. Festival price points: 520 and 1314 for couples (May 20), 618 for mid-year sales, 88/888 for Lunar New Year.
  7. Cents matter online: 0.88 and 0.68 endings work well on e-commerce and delivery apps.

Caveats

Test before you roll out. Mixed audiences may not notice, and some Cantonese speakers read 58 differently. Lucky pricing is a trust signal, not a substitute for value.
